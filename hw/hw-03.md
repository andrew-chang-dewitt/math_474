## 6

:::{.question}

> The shelf life, in days, for bottles of a certain prescribed medicine
> is a random variable having the density function
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x) = \begin{cases}
>          \frac{20000}{(x+100)^3}, &x > 0, \\
>          0,                       &\text{elsewhere}.
>        \end{cases} \tag{6.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> Find the probability that a bottle of this medicine will have a shelf
> life of \
> (a) at least 200 days; \
> (b) anywhere from 80 to 120 days. \

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

F(x) &= \mathbb{P}(X \leq x) = \int_{-\infty}^x f(t) dt &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{CDF from PDF \{def. 3.7, Walpole\}}} \\
F(x) &= \begin{cases}
          \int_0^x \frac{20000}{(t+100)^3} dt, &x > 0, \\
          0, &\text{elsewhere}.
        \end{cases} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{6.1\}}} \\
F(x) &= \begin{cases}
          1 - \frac{10000}{(x+100)^2}, &x > 0, \\
          0, &\text{elsewhere}.
        \end{cases} \tag{6.2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{CDF for f(x)}} \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X \geq 200) &= 1 - \mathbb{P}(X < 200) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) shelf life $\geq$ 200 days}} \\
              &\equiv 1 - \mathbb{P}(X \leq 200) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\leq$ is equivalent to < for continuous CDF}} \\
              &= 1 - F(200) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$F(x) = \mathbb{P}(X \leq x)$}} \\
              &= 1 - ( 1 - \frac{10000}{((200)+100)^2} ) \\
              &= \frac{1}{9} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 200) &\approx 0.111 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(80 < X < 120) &= \int_{80}^{120} f(t) dt &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) shelf life from 80 to 120 days}} \\
                         &= \int_{80}^{120} \frac{20000}{(x+100)^3} dt &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$80 > 0$, so ignore $x \leq 0$ branch}} \\
                         &= [-\frac{10000}{(x + 100)^2}]_{80}^{120} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{integrate}} \\
                         &= [(-\frac{10000}{(220)^2}) - (-\frac{10000}{(180)^2})] &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{expand}} \\
                         &= \frac{10000}{32400} -\frac{10000}{48400} = \frac{1000}{9801} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{simplify}} \\
\mathbb{P}(80 < X < 120) &\approx 0.102 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 7

:::{.question}

> The total number of hours, measured in units of 100 hours, that a
> family runs a vacuum cleaner over a period of one year is a
> continuous random variable X that has the density function
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x) = \begin{cases}
>          x,     &0 < x < 1, \\
>          2 - x, &1 \leq x < 2, \\
>          0,     &\text{elsewhere}.
>        \end{cases} \tag{7.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> Find the probability that over a period of one year, a
> family runs their vacuum cleaner \
> (a) less than 120 hours; \
> (b) between 50 and 100 hours.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

F(x) &= \mathbb{P}(X \leq x) = \int_{-\infty}^x f(t) dt \\
F(x) &= \begin{cases}
          \int_{-\infty}^x 0 dt,       &x \leq 0, \\
          \int_{-\infty}^x t dt,       &0 < x < 1, \\
          \int_{-\infty}^x (2 - t) dt, &1 \leq x < 2, \\
          1,                         &x \geq 2 \\
        \end{cases} \\
     &= \begin{cases}
          0,                                            &x \leq 0, \\
          \int_{-\infty}^x t dt,                          &0 < x < 1, \\
          \int_{1}^x (2 - t) dt - \int_0^1 t dt, &1 \leq x < 2, \\
          1,                                            &x \geq 2 \\
  \end{cases} \\
F(x) &= \begin{cases}
          0,                       &x \leq 0, \\
          \frac{1}{2}x^2,          &0 < x < 1, \\
          2x - \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 1, &1 \leq x < 2, \\
          1,                       &x \geq 2 \\
        \end{cases} \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X < 120) &\equiv \mathbb{P}(X \leq x) = F(1.2) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) less than 1.2 hundred hours}} \\
                    &= 2(1.2) - \frac{1}{2} (1.2)^2 - 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$1 \leq 1.2 < 2$}} \\
                    &= 2.4 - 0.72 - 1 \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 120) &= 0.68 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(0.5 < X < 1) &\equiv \mathbb{P}(X \leq 1) - \mathbb{P}(X \leq 0.5) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) between 0.5 \& 1 hundred hours}} \\
                        &= F(1) - F(0.5) \\
                        &= [2(1) - \frac{1}{2}(1)^2 - 1] - [\frac{1}{2}(0.5)^2] \\
                        &= 2 - 0.5 - 1 - 0.125 \\
\mathbb{P}(0.5 < X < 1) &= 0.375 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 14

:::{.question}

> The waiting time, in hours, between successive speeders spotted by a
> radar unit is a continuous random variable with cumulative
> distribution function
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
>
> F(x) = \begin{cases}
>          0,           &x < 0, \\
>          1 - e^{-8x}, &x \geq 0.
>        \end{cases} \tag{14.1}
>
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> Find the probability of waiting less than 12 minutes between
> successive speeders \
> (a) using the cumulative distribution function of `X`; \
> (b) using the probability density function of `X`.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\mathbb{P}(X \leq 0.2) &= F(0.2) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\text{(a) using CDF}} \\
                       &= 1 - e^{-8(0.2)} = 1 - e^{-1.6} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{14.1\}}} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 0.2) &\approx 0.798 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 0.2) &= \mathbb{P}(0 < X < 0.2) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) using PDF}} \\
                       &= \int_0^{0.2} f(x) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def 3.6, rule 3\}}} \\
                       &= [1 - e^{-8(0.2)}] &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{no need to bother finding $f(x)$, because $\int f(x) \equiv F(x)$}} \\
                       &= 1 - e^{-1.6} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 0.2) &\approx 0.798 \space_\blacksquare \\
\end{align*}
$$

## 32

:::{.question}

> The proportion of the budget for a certain type of industrial company
> that is allotted to environmental and pollution control is coming
> under scrutiny. A data collection project determines that the
> distribution of these proportions is given by
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(y) = \begin{cases}
>          5 (1 − y)^4, &0 \geq y \geq 1, \\
>          0,           &\text{elsewhere}.
>        \end{cases} \tag{32.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> (a) Verify that the above is a valid density function. \
> (b) What is the probability that a company chosen at random expends
> less than 10% of its budget on environmental and pollution controls? \
> (c) What is the probability that a company selected at random spends
> more than 50% of its budget on environmental and pollution controls?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

  f(y) &= 0, \text{ for all } y < 0 \land y > 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) valid pdf?}} \\
  f(0) &= 5(1 - 0)^4 = 5(1) = 5 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{check Walpole def 3.6, requirement 1}} \\
f(0.5) &= 5(1 - 0.5)^4 = 5(0.0625) = 0.3125 \\
  f(1) &= 5(1 - 1)^4 = 5(0) = 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{shows $0 \leq f(y) \leq 5$ for all $0 \leq y \leq 1$}} \\
  ... \\
  f(y) &\geq 0, \text{ for all } y \in \mathcal{R} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{req. 1 satisfied}} \\

1 &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y) dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{check Walpole def 3.6, requirement 2}} \\
  &= \int_{-\infty}^0 f(y) dy + \int_{0}^1 f(y) dy + \int_1^{\infty} f(y) dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$f(y)$ definied piecewise}} \\
  &= 0 + \int_{0}^1 f(y) dy + 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$f(y) = 0$ for all $y < 0 \land y > 1$}} \\
  &= \int_0^1 5(1 - y)^4 dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{32.1\}}} \\
  &= \int_0^1 5u^4 ( -du ) = -5 \int_0^1 u^4 du &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{substitute $u = 1 - y$, \& $\frac{du}{dy} = -1 \implies du = -dy$}} \\
  &= -5 [\frac{u^5}{5}]_0^1 = -5 [\frac{(1-1)^5}{5} - \frac{(1-0)^5}{5}] \tag{32.2} \\
  &= - [(0)^5 - (1)^5] = 1 - 0 \\
1 &= 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{req. 2 satisfied}} \\
f(y)\space &\text{is a pdf } \space_\blacksquare  &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{requirements 1 \& 2 satisfied from Walpole def. 3.6}} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X < 0.1) &= \mathbb{P}(0 < X < 0.1) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) probability $< 10\%$}} \\
                    &= \int_0^{0.1} f(y) dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def 3.6, req. 3\}}} \\
                    &= - [(1 - y)^5]_0^{0.1} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{32.2\} previously found integral}} \\
                    &= - [(1 - 0.1)^5 - (1 - 0)^5] = 1^5 - (1 - 0.1)^5 \\
                    &= 1 - (0.99)^5 \approx 1 - 0.951 \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 0.1) &\approx 0.049 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X > 0.5) &= \mathbb{P}(0.5 < X < 1) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) probability $> 50\%$}} \\
                    &= \int_{0.5}^1 f(y) dy = - [(1 - y)^5]_{0.5}^1 \\
                    &= - [(1 - 1)^5 - (1 - 0.5)^5] = (0.5)^5 - (0)^5 \\
\mathbb{P}(X > 0.5) &= 0.3125 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 36

:::{.question}

> On a laboratory assignment, if the equipment is working, the density
> function of the observed outcome, `X`, is
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x) = \begin{cases}
>          2(1 − x), &0 < x < 1, \\
>          0,        &\text{otherwise}.
>        \end{cases} \tag{36.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> (a) Calculate $P(X ≤ 1/3)$. \
> (b) What is the probability that `X` will exceed 0.5? \
> (c) Given that $X \geq 0.5$, what is the probability that
> `X` will be less than 0.75?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

u &= 1-x \implies du = -dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{u-sub used throughout}} \\
\int 2(1-x)\,dx &= \int 2u\,(-du) = -u^2 = -(1-x)^2 \tag{36.2} \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X \leq \tfrac{1}{3}) &= \int_0^{1/3} 2(1-x)\,dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) \{Walpole def. 3.6\}; $f(x)=0$ for $x \leq 0$}} \\
                                &= [-(1-x)^2]_0^{1/3} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{36.2\}}} \\
                                &= -(\tfrac{2}{3})^2 + (1)^2 = 1 - \tfrac{4}{9} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq \tfrac{1}{3}) &= \tfrac{5}{9} \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X > 0.5) &= \int_{0.5}^1 2(1-x)\,dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) $f(x)=0$ for $x > 1$, so upper bound is 1}} \\
                    &= [-(1-x)^2]_{0.5}^1 \\
                    &= -(0)^2 + (\tfrac{1}{2})^2 \\
\mathbb{P}(X > 0.5) &= \tfrac{1}{4} \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X < 0.75 \mid X \geq 0.5) &= \frac{\mathbb{P}(0.5 \leq X < 0.75)}{\mathbb{P}(X \geq 0.5)} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) \{Walpole def. 2.10\}}} \\
  &\equiv \frac{\mathbb{P}(0.5 < X < 0.75)}{\mathbb{P}(X > 0.5)} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{continuous rv: $\mathbb{P}(\text{point}) = 0$}} \\
\mathbb{P}(0.5 < X < 0.75) &= \int_{0.5}^{0.75} 2(1 - x) dx  \\
                           &= [-(1-x)^2]_{0.5}^{0.75} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{36.2\}}} \\
                           &= -(\tfrac{1}{4})^2 + (\tfrac{1}{2})^2 = \tfrac{1}{4} - \tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac{3}{16} \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 0.75 \mid X \geq 0.5) &= \frac{3/16}{1/4} \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 0.75 \mid X \geq 0.5) &= \tfrac{3}{4} \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 42

:::{.question}

> Let $X$ and $Y$ denote the lengths of life, in years, of two components
> in an electronic system. If the joint density function of these variables
> is
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x, y) = \begin{cases}
>             e^{-(x+y)}, &x > 0,\ y > 0, \\
>             0,          &\text{elsewhere,}
>           \end{cases} \tag{42.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> find $P(0 < X < 1 \mid Y = 2)$.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\mathbb{P}(0 < X < 1 \mid Y = 2) &= \int_0^1 f(x|y) dx = \int_0^1 \frac{f(x,y)}{h(y)} dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.11\}}} \tag{42.2} \\
\\
h(y) &= \int_{\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.10\}}} \\
     &= \int_0^{\infty} e^{-(x + y)} dx = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-x} e^{-y} dx = e^{-y} \int_0^{\infty} e^{-x} dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$y$ is constant for this integral}} \\
     &= e^{-y} [(-e^{\infty}) - (-e^0)] = e^{-y} [1 - 0] \\
h(y) &= e^{-y} \tag{42.3} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(0 < X < 1 \mid Y = 2) &= \int_0^1 \frac{e^{-(x + y)}}{e^{-y}} dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{42.1, 42.2, 42.3\}}} \\
                                 &= \int_0^1 \frac{e^{-x}e^{-y}}{e^{-y}} dx = \int_0^1 e^{-x} dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{simplify}} \\
                                 &= -e^{-x} \mid_0^1 = (-e^{-1}) - (-e^0) = 1 - e^{-1} \\
\mathbb{P}(0 < X < 1 \mid Y = 2) &\approx 0.632 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 45

:::{.question}

> Let $X$ denote the diameter of an armored electric cable and $Y$ denote
> the diameter of the ceramic mold that makes the cable. Both $X$ and $Y$
> are scaled so that they range between 0 and 1. Suppose that $X$ and $Y$
> have the joint density
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x, y) = \begin{cases}
>             \frac{1}{y}, &0 < x < y < 1, \\
>             0,           &\text{elsewhere.}
>           \end{cases} \tag{45.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> Find $P(X + Y > 1/2)$.

:::

to start, find the bounds of the region, `A`, where $X + Y = \frac{1}{2}$
& $0 < x < y < 1$ is true.

first, the lower bound of $X$:

$$
\begin{align*}

x + y > \frac{1}{2} \land 0 < x < y &\implies x > \frac{1}{2} - y \\
                                    &\implies x_{\text{min}}(y) = \text{max}(0, \frac{1}{2} - y)

\end{align*}
$$

from there, $A$ is only nonempty if & only if

$$
\begin{align*}

x_{\text{min}}(y) < y &= \text{max}(0,\frac{1}{2} - y) < y \\

\end{align*}
$$

then it follows that

$$
\begin{align*}

\frac{1}{2} - y < y \iff y > \frac{1}{4} \\

\end{align*}
$$

meaning the following ranges hold:

1. $\not\exists x \text{ if } y \geq \frac{1}{4}$,
2. $\frac{1}{4} < y \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2} - y > 0 \implies x \in (\frac{1}{2} 0 y, y)$, and
3. $\frac{1}{2} \leq y < 1 \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2} - y \leq 0 \implies x \in (0,y)$.

therefore the bounds of integration will be both of the following

$$
\begin{align*}

      &y \in (\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{2}), x \in (\frac{1}{2} - y, y) \\
\land &y \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1),           x \in (0,y) \tag{45.2} \\

\end{align*}
$$

finally, with the bounds of A found, the probability can be solved

$$
\begin{alignat*}{4}

P(X + Y > \frac{1}{2}) &= \int \int_A f(x,y) dx dy &\space&\space&\space&  &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.9\}}} \\
                       &= \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \int_{\frac{1}{2} - y}^y \frac{1}{y} dx dy
                       &+& \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 \int_{0}^y \frac{1}{y} dx dy &\space& &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{45.1,45.2\}}} \\
                       &= \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{y} \int_{\frac{1}{2} - y}^y dx dy
                       &+& \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 \frac{1}{y} \int_{0}^y dx dy &\space& &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$y$ is constant for inner integral}} \\
                       &= \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{y} [2y - \frac{1}{2}] dy
                       &+& \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 \frac{1}{y} [y - 0] dy \\
                       &= \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} [2 - \frac{1}{2y}] dy
                       &+& \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 1 dy \\
                       &= \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} 2 dy
                       &-& \int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{2y} dy
                       &+& \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 1 dy \\
                       &= [2y]_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
                       &-& \frac{1}{2}[\ln y]_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{2y}
                       &+& [y]_{\frac{1}{2}}^1 \\
                       &= [1 - \frac{1}{2}]
                       &-& \frac{1}{2}[\ln \frac{1}{2} -\ln \frac{1}{4}]
                       &+& [1 - \frac{1}{2}] \\
                       &\approx 1 - 0.347 \\
P(X + Y > \frac{1}{2}) &\approx 0.653 \space_\blacksquare

\end{alignat*}
$$

> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x, y) = \begin{cases}
>             2, &0 < x \leq y < 1, \\
>             0, &\text{elsewhere.}
>           \end{cases} \tag{47.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> (a) Determine if $X$ and $Y$ are independent. \
> (b) Find $P(1/4 < X < 1/2 \mid Y = 3/4)$.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

g(x) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) \{Walpole def. 3.10\}}} \\
     &= \int_x^1 2 \, dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$f(x,y) = 2$ only for $x \leq y < 1$}} \\
     &= [2y]_x^1 = 2 - 2x \\
g(x) &= 2(1-x), \quad 0 < x < 1 \tag{47.2} \\
\\

h(y) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.10\}}} \\
     &= \int_0^y 2 \, dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$f(x,y) = 2$ only for $0 < x \leq y$}} \\
h(y) &= 2y, \quad 0 < y < 1 \tag{47.3} \\
\\

g(x)h(y) &= 2(1-x) \cdot 2y = 4y(1-x) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{47.2, 47.3\}}} \\
g(\tfrac14)h(\tfrac34) &= 4(\tfrac34)(1-\tfrac14) = \tfrac{9}{4} \neq 2 = f(\tfrac14,\tfrac34) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{point where $g(x)h(y)$ != $f(x,y)$}} \\
X \text{ \& } Y &\text{ are not statistically independent} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.12\}}} \\
\\

f(x|y) &= \frac{f(x,y)}{h(y)} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) \{Walpole def. 3.11\}}} \\
       &= \frac{2}{2y} = \frac{1}{y}, \quad 0 < x \leq y &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{47.1, 47.3\}}} \\
f(x \mid y = \tfrac34) &= \frac{1}{3/4} = \frac{4}{3}, \quad 0 < x \leq \tfrac34 \tag{47.4} \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(\tfrac14 < X < \tfrac12 \mid Y = \tfrac34) &= \int_{1/4}^{1/2} f(x \mid y = \tfrac34) \, dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole def. 3.11\}}} \\
                                                      &= \int_{1/4}^{1/2} \tfrac{4}{3} \, dx &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{47.4\}}} \\
                                                      &= \tfrac{4}{3} [x]_{1/4}^{1/2} = \tfrac{4}{3} (\tfrac12 - \tfrac14) \\
\mathbb{P}(\tfrac14 < X < \tfrac12 \mid Y = \tfrac34) &= \tfrac13 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

> queens, and kings) of an ordinary deck of 52 playing cards. Let $X$ be
> the number of kings selected and $Y$ the number of jacks. Find
>

$$

|S| &= \binom{12}{3} = \frac{12!}{3!9!} &&

       &\quad x,y \geq 0,\ x+y \leq 3 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$3-x-y$ queens from 4}} \tag{51.1} \\

        = \frac{1 \cdot 1 \cdot 4}{220} = \frac{1}{55} \\

## 52

:::{.question}

> A coin is tossed twice. Let $Z$ denote the number of heads on the first
> toss and $W$ the total number of heads on the 2 tosses. If the coin is
> unbalanced and a head has a 40% chance of occurring, find
>
> (a) the joint probability distribution of `W` and `Z`; \
> (b) the marginal distribution of `W`; \
> (c) the marginal distribution of `Z`; \
> (d) the probability that at least 1 head occurs.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

p &= \mathbb{P}(\text{head}) = 0.4, \quad q = \mathbb{P}(\text{tail}) = 0.6 \\
\\

f(0,0) &= \mathbb{P}(Z=0, W=0) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) tosses independent \{Walpole theorem 2.11\}}} \\
       &= \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 1}=T) \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 2}=T) \\
       &= q \cdot q = (0.6)(0.6) = 0.36 \\
f(0,1) &= \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 1}=T) \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 2}=H) \\
       &= q \cdot p = (0.6)(0.4) = 0.24 \\
f(0,2) &= 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$Z=0 \implies$ toss 1$=T$, so $W \leq 1$}} \\
f(1,0) &= 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$Z=1 \implies$ toss 1$=H$, so $W \geq 1$}} \\
f(1,1) &= \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 1}=H)\mathbb{P}(\text{toss 2}=T) \\
       &= p \cdot q = (0.4)(0.6) = 0.24 \\
f(1,2) &= \mathbb{P}(\text{toss 1}=H)\mathbb{P}(\text{toss 2}=H) \\
       &= p \cdot p = (0.4)(0.4) = 0.16 \\
\\

f(w,z) &= \begin{cases}
            0.36, &w = 0, z = 0, \\
            0.24, &w = 1, z = 0, \\
            0.24, &w = 1, z = 1, \\
            0.16, &w = 2, z = 1, \\
            0,    &\text{elsewhere}.
          \end{cases} \tag{52.1} \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

h(w) &= \sum_z f(w,z) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) \{Walpole def. 3.10\}, row totals of \{52.1\}}} \\
h(0) &= 0.36 + 0 = 0.36 \\
h(1) &= 0.24 + 0.24 = 0.48 \\
h(2) &= 0 + 0.16 = 0.16 \\
h(w) &= \begin{cases}
          0.36, &w = 0, \\
          0.48, &w = 1, \\
          0.16, &w = 2.
        \end{cases} \tag{52.2} \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

g(z) &= \sum_w f(w,z) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) \{Walpole def. 3.10\}, column totals of \{52.1\}}} \\
g(0) &= 0.36 + 0.24 + 0 = 0.60 \\
g(1) &= 0 + 0.24 + 0.16 = 0.40 \\
g(z) &= \begin{cases}
          0.60, &z = 0, \\
          0.40, &z = 1.
        \end{cases} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{matches $q, p$, as expected for toss 1}}

\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}

\mathbb{P}(\text{at least 1 head}) &= \mathbb{P}(W \geq 1) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(W = 0) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(d)}} \\
                                   &= 1 - h(0) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{52.2\}}} \\
                                   &= 1 - 0.36 \\
\mathbb{P}(\text{at least 1 head}) &= 0.64 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$
