## 15

:::{.question}

> Consider the sample space and the events
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> S &= \{c, s, n, p, u, o, z\} \\
>   &\text{and} \\
> A &= \{c, s, z\}, \\
> B &= \{s, n, p\}, \\
> C &= \{o\}, \\
>   &\text{where} \\
>   &\quad c \gets copper \\
>   &\quad s \gets sodium \\
>   &\quad n \gets nitrogen \\
>   &\quad p \gets potassium \\
>   &\quad u \gets uranium \\
>   &\quad o \gets oxygen \\
>   &\quad z \gets zinc \\
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> list the elements of the sets corresponding to the following events:

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
(a) &&                   A′ &= S \setminus A \\
    &&                      &= \{c, s, n, p, u, o, z\} \setminus \{c, s, z\} \\
    &&                   A′ &= \{n,p,u,o\} \space_\blacksquare \tag{15.1} \\
(b) &&                   A ∪ C &= \{c,s,z,o\} \space_\blacksquare \\
(c) &&        (A ∩ B′) ∪ C′ &= (\{c, s, z\} \cap \{c, u, o, z\}) \\
    &&                      &\quad\htmlClass{hljs-comment}{
      \textit{by $B' = S \setminus \{s, n, p\} = \{c, u, o, z\} $}} \tag{15.2} \\
    &&                      &\quad\quad\quad \cup \{c, s, n, p, u, z\} \\
    &&                      &\quad\htmlClass{hljs-comment}{
      \textit{by $C' = S \setminus \{o\} = \{c, s, n, p, u, z\} $}} \tag{15.3} \\
    &&                      &= \{c,z\} \cup \{c, s, n, p, u, z\} \\
    &&        (A ∩ B′) ∪ C′ &= \{c, s, n, p, u, z\} \space_\blacksquare \\
(d) &&              B′ ∩ C′ &= \{c, u, o, z\} \cap \{c, s, n, p, u, z\} &&
        \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{15.2,15.3\}}} \\
    &&              B′ ∩ C′ &= \{c, u, z\} \space_\blacksquare \\
(e) &&            A ∩ B ∩ C &= \{c, s, z\} \cap \{s, n, p\} \cap \{o\} \\
    &&                      &= ( \{c, s, z\} \cap \{s, n, p\} ) \cap \{o\} \\
    &&                      &= \{s\} \cap \{o\} \\
    &&            A ∩ B ∩ C &= \empty \space_\blacksquare \\
(f) && (A′ ∪ B′) ∩ (A′ ∩ C) &= ( \{n,p,u,o\} \cup \{c, u, o, z\} ) &&
        \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{{15.1,15.2}}} \\
    &&                      &\quad\quad \cap ( \{n,p,u,o\} \cap \{o\} ) \\
    &&                      &= \{n,p,u,o,c,z\} \cap \{n,p,u,o\} \cap \{o\} \\
    && (A′ ∪ B′) ∩ (A′ ∩ C) &= \{o\} \space_\blacksquare

## 31

:::{.question}

> A witness to a hit-and-run accident told the police that the license
> number contained the letters RLH followed by 3 digits, the first of
> which was a 5. If the witness cannot recall the last 2 digits, but is
> certain that all 3 digits are diﬀerent, find the maximum number of
> automobile registrations that the police may have to check.

:::

Because 5 is already used, the 2nd to last digit must be chosen from 9
possible choices, then the last digit will be chosen from 8. Thus, the

## 36

:::{.question}

> **(a)** How many three-digit numbers can be formed from the digits 0, 1,
> 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 if each digit can be used only once? \
> **(b)** How many of these are odd numbers? \
> **(c)** How many are greater than 330?

:::

### part (a)

$$
\begin{align*}
&|\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}| = 7 \\
&7 * 6 * 5 = 210 \space_\blacksquare &&
 \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{sampling w/out replacement by Multiplication Rule}} \\
\end{align*}
$$

### part (b)

to be odd, the following must be true about
digits $d_1$, $d_2$, & $d_3$:

$$
\begin{align*}
d_3 \in \{1,3,5\} \\
d \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\} \setminus \{d_3\} \forall \{d_1,d_2\}
\end{align*}
$$

therefore the count of the set of numbers that are odd (`B`) is:

$$
\begin{align*}
|B| &= 6 * 5 * 3 \\
|B| &= 90 \space_\blacksquare
\end{align*}
$$

### part (c)

to be greater than `330`, the following must be true about
digits $d_1$, $d_2$, & $d_3$:

$$
\begin{align*}
&p \lor q, \text{where} \\
&\quad\quad p \equiv d_1 \in \{3\} \land d_2 \in \{4,5,6\} \land d_3 \in \{0,1,2,4,5,6\} \setminus \{d_2\} \\
&\quad\quad q \equiv d_1 \in \{4,5,6\} \land d \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\} \setminus \{d_1\} \forall \{d_2,d_3\} \\
\end{align*}
$$

therefore the count of the set of numbers greater than `330` (`C`) is:

$$
\begin{align*}
|C| &= 1 * 3 * 5 + 3 * 5 * 4 \\
    &= 15 + 60 \\
|C| &= 75 \space_\blacksquare
\end{align*}
$$

## 46

:::{.question}

> In how many ways can 3 oaks, 4 pines, and 2 maples be arranged along
> a property line if one does not distinguish among trees of the same
> kind?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
o &= 3 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{number of oaks}} \\
p &= 4 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{number of pines}} \\
m &= 2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{number of maples}} \\
n &= 3 + 4 + 2 = 9 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{total number of trees}} \\
S &= \begin{pmatrix}
       n \\
       o, p, m
     \end{pmatrix} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{by Theorem 2.5 [Walpole \& Myers, 9th ed.]}} \\
  &= \begin{pmatrix}
       9 \\
       3, 4, 2
     \end{pmatrix}
   = \frac{9!}{3! 4! 2!} \\
  &= \frac{9 * 8 * 7 * 6 * 5}{3 * 2 * 1 * 2 * 1} \\
  &= 9 * 7 * 5 * 4 = 63 * 20 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{simplify}} \\
  &= 1260 \space_\blacksquare
\end{align*}
$$

## 51

:::{.question}

> A box contains 500 envelopes, of which 75 contain $100 in cash, 150
> contain $25, and 275 contain $10. An envelope may be purchased for
> $25. What is the sample space for the different amounts of money?
> Assign probabilities to the sample points and then find the
> probability that the first envelope purchased contains less than $100.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
  S &= A \cup B \cup C, \text{where} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{sample space}} \\
|S| &= 500 \\
|A| &= 75 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{number of envelopes containing \$100}} \\
|B| &= 150 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...containing \$25}} \\
|C| &= 275 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...containing \$10}} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(A) &= \frac{|A|}{|S|} = \frac{75}{500} = 0.15 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{probability drawing one envelope contains \$100}} \\
\mathbb{P}(B) &= \frac{|B|}{|S|} = \frac{150}{500} = 0.30 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...contains \$25}} \\
\mathbb{P}(C) &= \frac{|C|}{|S|} = \frac{275}{500} = 0.55 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...contains \$10}} \\
\\
            D &= B \cup C = A' = S \setminus A &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{event first envelope purchased contains less than \$100}} \\
\mathbb{P}(D) &= \mathbb{P}(A') = 1 - \mathbb{P}(A) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{by Theorem 2.9 [Walpole \& Myers, 9th ed.]}} \\
              &= 1 - 0.15 \\
\mathbb{P}(D) &= 0.85 \space_\blacksquare
\end{align*}
$$

## 60

:::{.question}

> If 3 books are picked at random from a shelf containing 5 novels, 3
> books of poems, and a dictionary, what is the probability that \
> **(a)** the dictionary is selected? \
> **(b)** 2 novels and 1 book of poems are selected?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
n &= 9 \\
v &= 5 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{number of novels}} \\
p &= 3 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...poems}} \\
d &= 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{...dictionaries}} \\
\\
S &= \begin{pmatrix}
       n \\
       3
     \end{pmatrix} = 84, \text{where} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{sample space for selecting 3}} \\
            A &\gets \text{event 1 is a dictionary} \\
          |A| &= 1 * \begin{pmatrix}
                       8 \\
                       2
                     \end{pmatrix} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{1 must be dictionary, rest from 8}} \\
              &= \frac{8!}{2!6!} = \frac{8*7}{2*1} = 4*7 = 28 \\
\mathbb{P}(A) &= \frac{|A|}{|S|} = \frac{28}{84} = \frac{1}{3} \\
              &\approx 0.33 \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) probability of a dictionary}} \\
\\
B &\gets \text{event 2 novels \& 1 poems} \\
|B| &= \begin{pmatrix} v \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} * \begin{pmatrix} p \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\
    &= \frac{5!}{2!3!} * \frac{3!}{1!2!} \\
    &= \frac{5*4}{2*1} * \frac{3}{1} \\
    &= \frac{20}{2} * \frac{3}{1} \\
    &= 10 * 3 \\
|B| &= 30 \\
\mathbb{P}(B) &= \frac{|B|}{|S|} = \frac{30}{84} = \frac{5}{14} \\
              &\approx 0.36 \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) probability of 2 novels \& 1 poems}} \\
\end{align*}
$$

## 63

:::{.question}

> According to Consumer Digest (July/August 1996), the probable
> location of personal computers (PC) in the home is as follows:
>
> - Adult bedroom: 0.03 (`P`)
> - Child bedroom: 0.15 (`Q`)
> - Other bedroom: 0.14 (`R`)
> - Office or den: 0.40 (`T`)
> - Other rooms: 0.28 (`V`)
>
> **(a)** What is the probability that a PC is in a bedroom? \
> **(b)** What is the probability that it is not in a bedroom? \
> **(c)** Suppose a household is selected at random from households with a
> PC; in what room would you expect to find a PC?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
            A &= P \cup Q \cup R &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{event in a bedroom}} \\
\mathbb{P}(A) &= \mathbb{P}(P) + \mathbb{P}(Q) + \mathbb{P}(R) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{addition of disjoint events}} \\
              &= 0.03 + 0.15 + 0.14 \\
\mathbb{P}(A) &= 0.32 \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) probability PC in a bedroom}} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(A') &= 1 - \mathbb{P}(A) = 1 - 0.32 \\
\mathbb{P}(A') &= 0.68 \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) probability PC not in a bedroom}} \\
\\
&\text{most likely } \longrightarrow \text{highest probability} \\
&\text{most likely } \equiv \text{Office or den} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) most likely room}} \\
\end{align*}
$$

</section>
