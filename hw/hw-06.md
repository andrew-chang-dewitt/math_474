## 10.17

:::{.question}

> A new curing process developed for a certain type of cement results
> in a mean compressive strength of 5000 kilograms per square
> centimeter with a standard deviation of 120 kilograms. To test the
> hypothesis that $\mu = 5000$ against the alternative that $\mu < 5000$,
> a random sample of 50 pieces of cement is tested. The critical
> region is defined to be $\bar X < 4970$. \
> \
> (a) Find the probability of committing a type I error when `H0` is
> true. \
> (b) Evaluate $\beta$ for the alternatives $\mu = 4970$ and $\mu = 4960$.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\text{H0} &\longleftarrow \mu = 5000 \\
\text{H1} &\longleftarrow \mu < 5000 \\
\sigma &= 120 \text{kg} \\
n &= 50 > 30 \implies \bar X \sim N(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{sample size is large enough to be approx. normal}} \\
\\
\alpha &= \mathbb{P}(\bar X < 4970 | \mu = 5000) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) type I err.}} \\
       &= P(Z < z) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{cdf of standard normal distribution}} \\
z &= \frac{\bar X - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{standardize $\bar X$}} \\
  &= \frac{4970 - 5000}{120 / \sqrt{50}} \\
z &\approx -1.77 \\
\alpha &= \Phi(-1.77) \approx 0.0384 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\beta &= \mathbb{P}(\bar X \geq 4970 | \mu = \text{alt.}) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) type II err. for alts 4970 \& 4960}} \\
\text{when}\space \mu &= 4970 \\
              z &= 0 \implies \beta = \mathbb{P}(Z \geq 0) \\
\text{therefore}\space \beta &= 0.5 \space_\blacksquare \\
\text{when}\space \mu &= 4960 \\
              z &= \frac{4970 - 4960}{120 / \sqrt{50}} \\
              z &\approx 0.59 \implies \beta = \mathbb{P}(Z \geq 0.59) \\
\text{therefore}\space \beta &= 1 - \Phi(0.59) \approx 1 - 0.7224 \\
                       \beta &\approx 0.2776 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.25

:::{.question}

> It is claimed that automobiles are driven on average more than
> 20,000 kilometers per year. To test this claim, 100 randomly
> selected automobile owners are asked to keep a record of the
> kilometers they travel. Would you agree with this claim if the
> random sample showed an average of 23,500 kilometers and a standard
> deviation of 3900 kilometers? Use a P-value in your conclusion.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\text{H0:} \mu &= 20000 \\
\text{H1:} \mu &> 20000 \\
\\
\mu_0 &= 20000 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{null mean}} \\
\bar x &= 23500 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{sample mean}} \\
n &= 100 \\
s &= 3900 \\
\\
z &= \frac{23500 - 20000}{3900/\sqrt{100}} = \frac{3500}{390} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{test statistic for z-test}} \\
z &\approx 8.97 \\
\\
P &= P(Z > 8.97) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{one-tailed test of normal population}} \\
P &\approx 0 \implies \text{$\bar x$ is well outside critical region} \\
  &\text{reject $H0$} \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.27

:::{.question}

> A study at the University of Colorado at Boulder shows that running
> increases the percent resting metabolic rate (RMR) in older women.
> The average RMR of 30 elderly women runners was 34.0% higher than
> the average RMR of 30 sedentary elderly women, and the standard
> deviations were reported to be 10.5 and 10.2%, respectively. Was
> there a significant increase in RMR of the women runners over the
> sedentary women? Assume the populations to be approximately
> normally distributed with equal variances. Use a P-value in your
> conclusions.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\text{$\bar{X_1}$:}&\space\text{elderly women runners} \\
\text{$\bar{X_2}$:}&\space\text{sedentary elderly women} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{both assumed normally distributed}} \\
n_1 &= n_2 = 30 \\
s_1 &= 10.5\% \\
s_2 &= 10.2\% &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{variances are similar}} \\
\bar{x_1} &- \bar{x_2} = 34.0\% \\
d_0 &= 0.0\% \\
\\
\text{H0:} &\mu_1 = \mu_2 \\
\text{H1:} &\mu_1 - \mu_2 > 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{one-tailed hypothesis}} \\
\\
s_p^2 &= \frac{(n_1 - 1)s_1^2 + (n_2 - 1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pooled variance}} \\
      &= \frac{29(10.5^2)+29(10.2^2)}{58} \\
s_p^2 &\approx 107.1 \\
s_p &= \sqrt{s_p^2} \approx 10.35 \\
\\
t &= \frac{(\bar{x_1} - \bar{x_2}) - d_0}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{normal dist., sim. var. $\implies$ pooled t-test}} \\
  &\approx \frac{34.0 - 0}{10.35\sqrt{1/15}} \\
  &\approx \frac{34.0}{2.67} \\
t &\approx 12.7 \\
\\
P &= \mathbb{P}(T_{58} > 12.7) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{P-value to check if 12.7 is reasonable}} \\
P &\approx 0 \implies \space\text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{strong evidence that $X_1$'s RMR}} \\
&\space &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{is significantly higher than $X_2$'s}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.33

:::{.question}

> A study was conducted to see if increasing the substrate
> concentration has an appreciable effect on the velocity of a
> chemical reaction. With a substrate concentration of 1.5 moles per
> liter, the reaction was run 15 times, with an average velocity of
> 7.5 micromoles per 30 minutes and a standard deviation of 1.5. With
> a substrate concentration of 2.0 moles per liter, 12 runs were
> made, yielding an average velocity of 8.8 micromoles per 30 minutes
> and a sample standard deviation of 1.2. Is there any reason to
> believe that this increase in substrate concentration causes an
> increase in the mean velocity of the reaction of more than 0.5
> micromole per 30 minutes? Use a 0.01 level of significance and
> assume the populations to be approximately normally distributed
> with equal variances.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n_1 &= 15, \quad \bar x_1 = 7.5, \quad s_1 = 1.5 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{1.5 mol/L concentration}} \\
n_2 &= 12, \quad \bar x_2 = 8.8, \quad s_2 = 1.2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{2.0 mol/L concentration}} \\
d_0 &= 0.5 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ \mu_2 - \mu_1 &= 0.5 \\
\text{H1:}\ \mu_2 - \mu_1 &> 0.5 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{one-tailed, testing a specific effect size}} \\
\\
s_p^2 &= \frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{normal dist., equal var. $\implies$ pooled t-test}} \\
      &= \frac{14(1.5^2) + 11(1.2^2)}{25} \\
      &= \frac{31.5+15.84}{25} \\
s_p^2 &\approx 1.894 \\
s_p &= \sqrt{s_p^2} \approx 1.376 \\
\\
t &= \frac{(\bar x_2 - \bar x_1) - d_0}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{test statistic, note nonzero $d_0$}} \\
  &= \frac{(8.8-7.5)-0.5}{1.376\sqrt{1/15+1/12}} \\
  &\approx \frac{0.8}{0.533} \\
t &\approx 1.50 \\
v &= n_1+n_2-2 = 25 \\
\\
t_{0.01}(25) &\approx 2.485 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\alpha = 0.01$ given, compare to critical value}} \\
t &\approx 1.50 < 2.485 \\
  &\implies \text{fail to reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{insufficient evidence increase exceeds 0.5}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.37

:::{.question}

> In Exercise 9.42, test the hypothesis that the fuel economy of
> Volkswagen mini-trucks, on average, exceeds that of similarly
> equipped Toyota mini-trucks by 4 kilometers per liter. Use a 0.10
> level of significance. \
> \
> Exercise 9.42: An experiment reported in _Popular Science_ compared
> fuel economies for two types of similarly equipped diesel
> mini-trucks. Suppose that 12 Volkswagen and 10 Toyota trucks were
> tested in 90-kilometer-per-hour steady-paced trials. The 12
> Volkswagen trucks averaged 16 kilometers per liter with a standard
> deviation of 1.0 kilometer per liter and the 10 Toyota trucks
> averaged 11 kilometers per liter with a standard deviation of 0.8
> kilometer per liter. Assume that the distances per liter for the
> truck models are approximately normally distributed with equal
> variances.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n_1 &= 12, \quad \bar x_1 = 16, \quad s_1 = 1.0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{Volkswagen}} \\
n_2 &= 10, \quad \bar x_2 = 11, \quad s_2 = 0.8 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{Toyota}} \\
d_0 &= 4 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ \mu_1 - \mu_2 &= 4 \\
\text{H1:}\ \mu_1 - \mu_2 &> 4 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{one-tailed, testing a specific effect size}} \\
\\
s_p^2 &= \frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{normal dist., equal var. $\implies$ pooled t-test}} \\
      &= \frac{11(1.0^2) + 9(0.8^2)}{20} = \frac{11+5.76}{20} \\
s_p^2 &\approx 0.838 \\
s_p &= \sqrt{s_p^2} \approx 0.915 \\
\\
t &= \frac{(\bar x_1 - \bar x_2) - d_0}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{test statistic, note nonzero $d_0$}} \\
  &= \frac{(16-11)-4}{0.915\sqrt{1/12+1/10}} \\
  &\approx \frac{1}{0.392} \\
t &\approx 2.55 \\
v &= n_1+n_2-2 = 20 \\
\\
t_{0.10}(20) &\approx 1.325 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\alpha = 0.10$ given, compare to critical value}} \\
t &\approx 2.55 > 1.325 \implies \text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{VW exceeds Toyota by more than 4 km/L}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.45

:::{.question}

> A taxi company manager is trying to decide whether the use of
> radial tires instead of regular belted tires improves fuel economy.
> Twelve cars were equipped with radial tires and driven over a
> prescribed test course. Without changing drivers, the same cars
> were then equipped with regular belted tires and driven once again
> over the test course. The gasoline consumption, in kilometers per
> liter, was recorded as follows:
>
> | Car | Radial Tires | Belted Tires |
> | --- | ------------ | ------------ |
> | 1   | 4.2          | 4.1          |
> | 2   | 4.7          | 4.9          |
> | 3   | 6.6          | 6.2          |
> | 4   | 7.0          | 6.9          |
> | 5   | 6.7          | 6.8          |
> | 6   | 4.5          | 4.4          |
> | 7   | 5.7          | 5.7          |
> | 8   | 6.0          | 5.8          |
> | 9   | 7.4          | 6.9          |
> | 10  | 4.9          | 4.7          |
> | 11  | 6.1          | 6.0          |
> | 12  | 5.2          | 4.9          |
>
> Can we conclude that cars equipped with radial tires give better
> fuel economy than those equipped with belted tires? Assume the
> populations to be normally distributed. Use a P-value in your
> conclusion.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

d_i &= \text{radial}_i - \text{belted}_i &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{same 12 cars $\implies$ paired data}} \\
d_i &= 0.1, -0.2, 0.4, 0.1, -0.1, 0.1, \\
    &\quad 0.0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.3 \\
\\
\bar d &= \frac{\sum d_i}{n} = \frac{1.7}{12} \approx 0.1417 \\
s_d &= \sqrt{\frac{\sum (d_i - \bar d)^2}{n-1}} \approx 0.1975 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{ch1 \S1.3/1.4, applied to the differences}} \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ \mu_D &= 0 \\
\text{H1:}\ \mu_D &> 0 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{radial gives higher km/L than belted, one-tailed}} \\
\\
t &= \frac{\bar d - 0}{s_d/\sqrt n} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S10.5\} paired t-test}} \\
  &= \frac{0.1417}{0.1975/\sqrt{12}} \\
t &\approx 2.48 \\
v &= n - 1 = 11 \\
\\
P &= \mathbb{P}(T_{11} > 2.48) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{one-tailed P-value}} \\
P &\approx 0.017 \implies \text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{radial tires give better fuel economy}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.57

:::{.question}

> A new radar device is being considered for a certain missile
> defense system. The system is checked by experimenting with
> aircraft in which a kill or a no kill is simulated. If, in 300
> trials, 250 kills occur, accept or reject, at the 0.04 level of
> significance, the claim that the probability of a kill with the new
> system does not exceed the 0.8 probability of the existing device.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n &= 300, \quad x = 250, \quad p_0 = 0.8, \quad q_0 = 0.2 \\
\hat p &= x/n = 250/300 \approx 0.833 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ p &= 0.8 \\
\text{H1:}\ p &> 0.8 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{does data show it actually exceeds 0.8?}} \\
\\
z &= \frac{x - np_0}{\sqrt{np_0 q_0}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S10.8\} z-test, single proportion}} \\
  &= \frac{250 - 240}{\sqrt{300(0.8)(0.2)}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt{48}} \\
z &\approx 1.44 \\
\\
z_{0.04} &\approx 1.75 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\alpha = 0.04$ given, compare to critical value}} \\
z &\approx 1.44 < 1.75 \implies \text{fail to reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{insufficient evidence kill prob. exceeds 0.8}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.61

:::{.question}

> In a winter of an epidemic flu, the parents of 2000 babies were
> surveyed by researchers at a well-known pharmaceutical company to
> determine if the company's new medicine was effective after two
> days. Among 120 babies who had the flu and were given the medicine,
> 29 were cured within two days. Among 280 babies who had the flu but
> were not given the medicine, 56 recovered within two days. Is there
> any significant indication that supports the company's claim of the
> effectiveness of the medicine?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n_1 &= 120, \quad x_1 = 29 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{given medicine}} \\
n_2 &= 280, \quad x_2 = 56 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{not given medicine}} \\
\hat p_1 &= 29/120 \approx 0.242, \quad \hat p_2 = 56/280 = 0.20 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ p_1 &= p_2 \\
\text{H1:}\ p_1 &> p_2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{medicine claimed effective, one-tailed}} \\
\\
\hat p &= \frac{x_1+x_2}{n_1+n_2} = \frac{85}{400} \approx 0.213, \quad \hat q \approx 0.787 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pooled estimate, since H0 asserts common $p$}} \\
\\
z &= \frac{\hat p_1 - \hat p_2}{\sqrt{\hat p \hat q (1/n_1 + 1/n_2)}} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S10.9\} z-test, two proportions}} \\
  &= \frac{0.242 - 0.20}{\sqrt{(0.213)(0.787)(1/120+1/280)}} \\
z &\approx 0.93 \\
\\
P &= \mathbb{P}(Z > 0.93) = 1 - 0.8238 \\
P &\approx 0.176 \implies \text{fail to reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{no significant evidence medicine is effective}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.65

:::{.question}

> An urban community would like to show that the incidence of breast
> cancer is higher in their area than in a nearby rural area. (PCB
> levels were found to be higher in the soil of the urban community.)
> If it is found that 20 of 200 adult women in the urban community
> have breast cancer and 10 of 150 adult women in the rural community
> have breast cancer, can we conclude at the 0.05 level of
> significance that breast cancer is more prevalent in the urban
> community?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n_1 &= 200, \quad x_1 = 20 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{urban}} \\
n_2 &= 150, \quad x_2 = 10 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{rural}} \\
\hat p_1 &= 20/200 = 0.10, \quad \hat p_2 = 10/150 \approx 0.0667 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ p_1 &= p_2 \\
\text{H1:}\ p_1 &> p_2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{urban rate claimed higher, one-tailed}} \\
\\
\hat p &= \frac{x_1+x_2}{n_1+n_2} = \frac{30}{350} \approx 0.0857, \quad \hat q \approx 0.9143 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pooled estimate, since H0 asserts common $p$}} \\
\\
z &= \frac{\hat p_1 - \hat p_2}{\sqrt{\hat p \hat q (1/n_1 + 1/n_2)}} &&

z &\approx 1.10 \\
\\
z_{0.05} &\approx 1.645 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\alpha = 0.05$ given, compare to critical value}} \\
z &\approx 1.10 < 1.645 \implies \text{fail to reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{insufficient evidence urban rate is higher}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.69

:::{.question}

> Aflotoxins produced by mold on peanut crops in Virginia must be
> monitored. A sample of 64 batches of peanuts reveals levels of 24.17
> ppm, on average, with a variance of 4.25 ppm. Test the hypothesis
> that `σ² = 4.2` ppm against the alternative that `σ² ≠ 4.2` ppm. Use
> a P-value in your conclusion.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n &= 64, \quad s^2 = 4.25, \quad \sigma_0^2 = 4.2 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ \sigma^2 &= 4.2 \\
\text{H1:}\ \sigma^2 &\neq 4.2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{two-tailed}} \\
\\
\chi^2 &= \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S10.10, thm 8.4\} $\chi^2$-test, single variance}} \\
    &= \frac{63(4.25)}{4.2} \\
\chi^2 &\approx 63.75 \\
v &= n - 1 = 63 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{note $\chi^2 \approx v$, i.e. near center of dist.}} \\
\\
P &= 2\min\big(\mathbb{P}(\chi^2_{63} > 63.75), \\
  &\quad\quad\ \ \mathbb{P}(\chi^2_{63} < 63.75)\big) \\
P &\gg 0.20 \implies \text{fail to reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{data consistent with claimed $\sigma^2 = 4.2$}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.79

> A machine is supposed to mix peanuts, hazelnuts, cashews, and pecans
> in the ratio 5:2:2:1. A can containing 500 of these mixed nuts was
> found to have 269 peanuts, 112 hazelnuts, 74 cashews, and 45 pecans.

> machine is mixing the nuts in the ratio 5:2:2:1.

$$
\begin{align*}

n &= 500, \quad p_i = \tfrac{5}{10}, \tfrac{2}{10}, \tfrac{2}{10}, \tfrac{1}{10} &&

e_i &= np_i = 250, 100, 100, 50 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{peanuts, hazelnuts, cashews, pecans}} \\
o_i &= 269, 112, 74, 45 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ &\text{nuts mixed in ratio } 5:2:2:1 \\

\chi^2 &\approx 10.14 \\

\\
\chi^2_{0.05}(3) &\approx 7.815 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\alpha = 0.05$ given, compare to critical value}} \\
\chi^2 &\approx 10.14 > 7.815 \implies \text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{machine not mixing nuts in the claimed ratio}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 10.93

:::{.question}

> To determine current attitudes about prayer in public schools, a
> survey was conducted in four Virginia counties. The following table
> gives the attitudes of 200 parents from Craig County, 150 parents
> from Giles County, 100 parents from Franklin County, and 100
> parents from Montgomery County:
>
> | Attitude   | Craig | Giles | Franklin | Mont. |
> | ---------- | ----- | ----- | -------- | ----- |
> | Favor      | 65    | 66    | 40       | 34    |
> | Oppose     | 42    | 30    | 33       | 42    |
> | No opinion | 93    | 54    | 27       | 24    |
>
> Test for homogeneity of attitudes among the four counties concerning
> prayer in the public schools. Use a P-value in your conclusion.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

N &= 550 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{fixed county sample sizes $\implies$ homogeneity test}} \\
\text{tot}_{\text{row}} &\coloneqq \begin{matrix} \{ &\text{Favor}=205, \\
                                                     &\text{Oppose}=147, \\
                                                     &\text{No op.}=198 \}
                                   \end{matrix} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{row totals}} \\
\text{tot}_{\text{col}} &\coloneqq \begin{matrix} \{ &\text{Craig}=200, \\
                                                     &\text{Giles}=150, \\
                                                     &\text{Franklin}=100, \\
                                                     &\text{Mont.}=100 \}
                                   \end{matrix} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{column totals}} \\
\\
i &\in [1,4),\quad j \in [1,3) \\
\text{H0:}\ &e_{i,j} \sim e_{i+1,j+1} \forall i, j &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{attitude proportions are the same across counties}} \\
\text{H1:}\ &\exist i, j \space\text{s.t.}\space e_{i,j} \not \sim e_{i+1,j+1} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{attitude proportions are not all the same}} \\
\\
e_{ij} &= \frac{(\text{tot}_{\text{row}_i})(\text{tot}_{\text{col}_j})}{N} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S10.13\} $\chi^2$-test, homogeneity}} \\
e_{\text{Favor}} &= 74.5, 55.9, 37.3, 37.3 \\
e_{\text{Oppose}} &= 53.5, 40.1, 26.7, 26.7 \\
e_{\text{No op.}} &= 72.0, 54.0, 36.0, 36.0 \\
\\
\chi^2 &= \sum_{i,j} \frac{(o_{ij}-e_{ij})^2}{e_{ij}} \\
       &\approx 3.53 + 15.20 + 12.38 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{summed by row (Favor, Oppose, No op.)}} \\
\chi^2 &\approx 31.10 \\
v &= (r-1)(c-1) = (2)(3) = 6 \\
\\
P &= \mathbb{P}(\chi^2_6 > 31.10) \\
P &< 0.001 \implies \text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{attitudes are not homogeneous across counties}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

</section>

## 10.101

:::{.question}

> In a study analyzed by the Statistics Consulting Center at Virginia
> Tech, a group of subjects was asked to complete a certain task on
> the computer. The response measured was the time to completion. The
> purpose of the experiment was to test a set of facilitation tools
> developed by the Department of Computer Science at the university.
> There were 10 subjects involved. With a random assignment, five
> were given a standard procedure using Fortran language for
> completion of the task. The other five were asked to do the task
> with the use of the facilitation tools. The data on the completion
> times for the task are given here.
>
> | Group 1 (Standard Procedure) | Group 2 (Facilitation Tool) |
> | ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
> | 161                          | 132                         |
> | 169                          | 162                         |
> | 174                          | 134                         |
> | 158                          | 138                         |
> | 163                          | 133                         |
>
> Assuming that the population distributions are normal and variances
> are the same for the two groups, support or refute the conjecture
> that the facilitation tools increase the speed with which the task
> can be accomplished.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n_1 &= n_2 = 5 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{random assignment $\implies$ independent samples}} \\
\bar x_1 &= \frac{161+169+174+158+163}{5} = 165 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{Group 1, standard procedure}} \\
\bar x_2 &= \frac{132+162+134+138+133}{5} = 139.8 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{Group 2, facilitation tool}} \\
s_1^2 &\approx 41.5, \quad s_2^2 \approx 159.2 \\
\\
\text{H0:}\ \mu_1 &= \mu_2 \\
\text{H1:}\ \mu_1 &> \mu_2 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{tool speeds up (lowers) completion time}} \\
\\
s_p^2 &= \frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{normal dist., equal var. $\implies$ pooled t-test}} \\
      &= \frac{4(41.5) + 4(159.2)}{8} = \frac{802.8}{8} \\
s_p^2 &\approx 100.35 \\
s_p &= \sqrt{s_p^2} \approx 10.02 \\
\\
t &= \frac{\bar x_1 - \bar x_2}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}} \\
  &= \frac{165 - 139.8}{10.02\sqrt{1/5+1/5}} \\
t &\approx 3.98 \\
v &= n_1+n_2-2 = 8 \\
\\
P &= \mathbb{P}(T_8 > 3.98) \\
P &\approx 0.002 \implies \text{reject H0} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{tools increase task-completion speed}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

</section>
