## 5.2

:::{.question}

> Twelve people are given two identical speakers, which they are asked
> to listen to for differences, if any. Suppose that these people
> answer simply by guessing. Find the probability that three people
> claim to have heard a difference between the two speakers.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n &= 12, \quad p = \tfrac12, \quad x = 3 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{guessing implies prob. $\frac12$}} \\
\\

\mathbb{P}(X = 3) &= b(3; 12, \tfrac12) = \binom{12}{3} \left(\tfrac12\right)^3 \left(\tfrac12\right)^{12-3} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{binomial distribution, Walpole \S5.2\}}} \\
                  &= \binom{12}{3} \left(\tfrac12\right)^{12} \\
                  &= (220) \left(\tfrac{1}{4096}\right) \\
                  &= \frac{220}{4096} = \frac{55}{1024} \\
\mathbb{P}(X = 3) &\approx 0.0537 \space_\blacksquare \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.7

:::{.question}

> One prominent physician claims that 70% of those with lung cancer are
> chain smokers. If his assertion is correct, \
> \
> (a) find the probability that of 10 such patients recently admitted
> to a hospital, fewer than half are chain smokers; \
> (b) find the probability that of 20 such patients recently admitted
> to a hospital, fewer than half are chain smokers.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\mathbb{P}(X < 5) &= \mathbb{P}(X \leq 4) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) prob of less than 5 is same as}} \\
                  &= B(4; 10, 0.70) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\quad\quad\textit{less than or equal 4}} \\
                  &= \sum_{x = 0}^4 b(x; 10, 0.70) = \sum_{x = 0}^4 \binom{10}{x} 0.70^x 0.30^{10 - x} \\
                  &= \frac{10!}{10!0!}(1)(0.30^10) + \frac{10!}{9!1!}(0.70^1)(0.30^9) \\
                  &\space + \frac{10!}{8!2!}(0.70^2)(0.30^8) + \frac{10!}{7!3!}(0.70^3)(0.30^7) \\
                  &\space + \frac{10!}{6!4!}(0.70^4)(0.30^6) \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 5) &\approx 0.04735 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X < 10) &= \mathbb{P}(X \leq 9) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) prob of less than 10 is same as}} \\
                   &= B(9; 20, 0.70) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\quad\quad\textit{less than or equal 9}} \\
                   &= \sum_{x = 0}^{9} b(x; 20, 0.70) = \sum_{x = 0}^{9} \binom{20}{x} 0.70^x 0.30^{20 - x} \\
\mathbb{P}(X < 10) &\approx 0.01714 \space_\blacksquare

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.13

:::{.question}

> A national study that examined attitudes about antidepressants
> revealed that approximately 70% of respondents believe
> "antidepressants do not really cure anything, they just cover up the
> real trouble." According to this study, what is the probability that
> at least 3 of the next 5 people selected at random will hold this
> opinion?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

\mathbb{P}(X \geq 3) &= 1 - \mathbb{P}(X \leq 2) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{complementary events}} \\
                     &= 1 - B(2; 5, 0.70) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{up to 2 of 5 is Bernoulli sum}} \\
                     &= 1 - \sum_{x = 0}^2 b(x; 5, 0.70) \\
                     &= 1 - \sum_{x = 0}^2 \binom{5}{x} 0.70^x 0.30^{5 - x} \\
                     &= 1 - \bigg[ \binom{5}{0} (1) 0.30^5 \\
                     &\quad\quad + \space \binom{5}{1} (0.70) 0.30^4 \\
                     &\quad\quad + \space \binom{5}{2} (0.70^2) 0.30^3 \bigg] \\
                     &\approx 1 - 0.1631 \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 3) &= 0.8369 \space_\blacksquare

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.19

:::{.question}

> As a student drives to school, he encounters a traffic signal. This
> traffic signal stays green for 35 seconds, yellow for 5 seconds, and
> red for 60 seconds. Assume that the student goes to school each
> weekday between 8:00 and 8:30 a.m. Let $X_1$ be the number of times

> encounters a yellow light, and $X_3$ be the number of times he
> encounters a red light. Find the joint distribution of $X_1$, $X_2$,
> and $X_3$.

$$
\begin{align*}

p_i &= \frac{\text{time in color}}{\text{total time}} \\

\end{align*}

## 5.27

:::{.question}

> If the probability that a fluorescent light has a useful life of at
> least 800 hours is 0.9, find the probabilities that among 20 such
> lights \
> \
> (a) exactly 18 will have a useful life of at least 800 hours; \
> (b) at least 15 will have a useful life of at least 800 hours; \
> (c) at least 2 will not have a useful life of at least 800 hours.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

      p &= 0.9 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{prob life $\geq$ 800 hours}} \\
      n &= 20 \\
   f(x) &= \binom{20}{x} 0.9^x 0.1^{20 - x} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pmf is binomial distribution}} \\
F(X = x) &= \sum_{k = 0}^x \binom{20}{k} 0.9^k 0.1^{20 - k} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{cdf is binomial sum}} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X = 18) &= f(18) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) exactly 18 last $\geq$ 800 hours}} \\
                   &= \binom{20}{18} 0.9^{18} 0.1^2 \\
\mathbb{P}(X = 18) &\approx 0.2852 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 15) &= 1 - \mathbb{P}(X \leq 14) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) at least 15 last $\geq$ 800 hours}} \\
                      &= 1 - F(14) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{use cdf}} \\
                      &= 1 - \sum_{k = 0}^{14} \binom{20}{k} 0.9^k 0.1^{20 - k} \\
                      &\approx 1 - 0.01125 \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 15) &\approx 0.9887 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 18) &= F(18) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) at least 2 do not last $\geq$ 800 hours}} \\
                      &= \sum_{k = 0}^{18} \binom{20}{k} 0.9^k 0.1^{20 - k} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 18) &\approx 0.6083 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.35

:::{.question}

> A company is interested in evaluating its current inspection
> procedure for shipments of 50 identical items. The procedure is to
> take a sample of 5 and pass the shipment if no more than 2 are found
> to be defective. What proportion of shipments with 20% defectives
> will be accepted?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

f(x) &= h(x; 50, 5, 10) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pmf of defective in sample is hypergeometric}} \\
f(x) &= \frac{\binom{10}{x}\binom{40}{5 - x}}{\binom{50}{5}} \\
F(X = x) &= \sum_{i = 0}^x f(i) \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 2) &= F(X = 2) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{probability of shipment passing w/ 20\% defective is}} \\
                  &\space &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{no more than 2 defective}} \\
                  &= \sum_{k = 0}^2 \frac{\binom{10}{k}\binom{40}{5 - k}}{\binom{50}{5}} \\
                  &= \frac{\binom{10}{0}\binom{40}{5 - 0}}{\binom{50}{5}} \\
                  &\space + \frac{\binom{10}{1}\binom{40}{5 - 1}}{\binom{50}{5}} \\
                  &\space + \frac{\binom{10}{2}\binom{40}{5 - 2}}{\binom{50}{5}} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 2) &\approx 0.9517 \space_\blacksquare

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.53

:::{.question}

> An inventory study determines that, on average, demands for a
> particular item at a warehouse are made 5 times per day. What is the
> probability that on a given day this item is requested \
> \
> (a) more than 5 times? \
> (b) not at all?

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

f(x) &= p(x; 5) = \frac{e^{-5} 5^x}{x!} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pmf is Poisson distribution}} \\
F(X) &= P(r; 5) = \sum_{x = 0}^r p(x; 5) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{cdf is Poisson sum}} \\
\\
P(X > 5) &= 1 - P(X \leq 5) = 1 - F(5) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) $> 5$ is complement of $\leq 5$}} \\
         &= 1 - \sum_{x = 0}^5 \frac{e^{-5} 5^x}{x!} \\
         &= 1 - e^{-5} \big[\frac{5^0}{0!} + \frac{5^1}{1!} + \frac{5^2}{2!} + \frac{5^3}{3!} + \frac{5^4}{4!} + \frac{5^5}{5!} \big] \\
         &= 1 - e^{-5} \big[1 + 5 + \frac{25}{2} + \frac{125}{6} + \frac{625}{24} + \frac{3125}{120} \big] \\
         &approx 1 - 0.006738 \cdot 91.42 \\
         &approx 1 - 0.6160 \\
P(X > 5) &\approx 0.3840 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
P(X = 0) &= f(0) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) not at all is $X = 0$}} \\
         &= \frac{e^{-5} 5^0}{0!} = e^{-5} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{$\frac{5^0}{0!} = \frac11 = 1$}} \\
P(X = 0) &\approx 0.006738 \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.67

> mean $\lambda = 7$. \

> (a) Compute the probability that more than 10 customers will arrive

> (b) What is the mean number of arrivals during a 2-hour period?

$$

\mu &= \lambda t = 7 (2) = 14 \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) mean of Poisson is $\lambda t$, where $t = 2$}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 5.101

:::{.question}

> The manufacturer of a tricycle for children has received complaints
> about defective brakes in the product. According to the design of the
> product and considerable preliminary testing, it had been determined
> that the probability of the kind of defect in the complaint was 1 in
> 10,000 (i.e., 0.0001). After a thorough investigation of the
> complaints, it was determined that during a certain period of time,
> 200 products were randomly chosen from production and 5 had defective
> brakes. \
> \
> (a) Comment on the "1 in 10,000" claim by the manufacturer. Use a
> probabilistic argument. Use the binomial distribution for your
> calculations. \
> (b) Repeat part (a) using the Poisson approximation.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

n &= 200 \\
p &= 0.0001 \\
f(x) &= b(x; 200, 0.0001) \\
     &= \binom{200}{x} 0.0001^x 0.9999^{200 - x} \\
F(X) &= B(x; 200, 0.0001) \\
     &= \sum_{x=0}^{X} \binom{200}{x} 0.0001^x 0.9999^{200 - x} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 5) &= 1 - F(4) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) 5 failures in 200 if claim is true}} \\
                     &= 1 - \sum_{x=0}^{4} \binom{200}{x} 0.0001^x 0.9999^{200 - x} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{4-decimal rounding hides the tail here}} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 5) &\approx 2.6 \times 10^{-11} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{it is highly improbable the claim is true}} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 5) &= 1 - P(5; 0.02) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) using Poisson where $\lambda = np = 200(0.0001)$}} \\
                     &= p(5; 0.02) = \frac{e^{-0.02}0.02^5}{5!} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{tail is dominated by the $x = 5$ term}} \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 5) &\approx 2.6 \times 10^{-11} \space_\blacksquare &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{same answer}} \\

\end{align*}
$$

## 6.3

:::{.question}

> The daily amount of coffee, in liters, dispensed by a machine located
> in an airport lobby is a random variable $X$ having a continuous
> uniform distribution with $A = 7$ and $B = 10$. Find the probability
> that on a given day the amount of coffee dispensed by this machine
> will be \
> (a) at most 8.8 liters; \
> (b) more than 7.4 liters but less than 9.5 liters; \
> (c) at least 8.5 liters.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

f(x) &= \begin{cases}
          \frac1{10 - 7}, &7 \leq x \leq 10 \\
          0,              &\text{elsewhere}
        \end{cases}
      = \begin{cases}
          \frac13, &7 \leq x \leq 10 \\
          0,       &\text{elsewhere}
        \end{cases} &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{pmf as continuous uniform distribution}} \\
F(X) &= \int_7^X \frac13 dx, \space\text{where}\space 7 \leq X \leq 10 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{cdf is integral over range}} \\
     &= \frac{x}3 \mid_{7}^X = \frac{X - 7}3, \space\text{therefore} \\
F(X) &= \begin{cases}
          \frac{X - 7}3, &7 \leq x \leq 10 \\
          0,             &\text{elsewhere}
        \end{cases} \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 8.8) &= F(8.8) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) at most 8.8 liters}} \\
                       &= \frac{8.8 - 7}3 = \frac{1.8}3 \\
\mathbb{P}(X \leq 8.8) &= 0.6 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(7.4 \leq X \leq 9.5) &= F(9.5) - F(7.4) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) from 7.4 to 9.5 liters}} \\
                                &= \frac{9.5 - 7.4}3 = \frac{2.1}3 \\
\mathbb{P}(7.4 \leq X \leq 9.5) &= 0.7 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 8.5) &= 1 - \mathbb{P}(X \leq 8.5) = 1 - F(8.5) &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(c) at least 8.5 liters}} \\
                       &= 1 - \frac{1.5}3 = 1 - 0.5 \\
\mathbb{P}(X \geq 8.5) &= 0.5 \space_\blacksquare \\
\\

\end{align*}
$$

## 7.9

:::{.question}

> The hospital period, in days, for patients following treatment for a

> where $X$ has the density function
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x) = \begin{cases}
>          \frac{32}{(x+4)^3}, &x > 0, \\
>          0,                  &\text{elsewhere}.
>        \end{cases} \tag{9.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> (a) Find the probability density function of the random variable $Y$. \
> (b) Using the density function of $Y$, find the probability that the
> hospital period for a patient following this treatment will exceed 8
> days.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

Y = X + 4 &\implies X = Y - 4 = w(y), \quad J = w'(y) = 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S7.2, theorem 7.3\} 1-1 transform}} \\
\\
g(y) &= f[w(y)]|J| &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(a) pdf of $Y$}} \\
     &= \frac{32}{((y-4)+4)^3}(1) \\
g(y) &= \begin{cases}
          \frac{32}{y^3}, &y > 4, \\
          0,              &\text{elsewhere}.
        \end{cases} \space_\blacksquare \\
\\
\mathbb{P}(Y > 8) &= \int_8^{\infty} \frac{32}{y^3} \, dy &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{(b) prob. hospital period exceeds 8 days}} \\
                  &= 32\left[-\frac{1}{2y^2}\right]_8^{\infty} \\
                  &= 32\left(0 - \left(-\frac{1}{128}\right)\right) \\
                  &= \frac{32}{128} \\
\mathbb{P}(Y > 8) &= 0.25 \space_\blacksquare

\end{align*}
$$

## 7.11

:::{.question}

> The amount of kerosene, in thousands of liters, in a tank at the
> beginning of any day is a random amount $Y$ from which a random
> amount $X$ is sold during that day. Assume that the joint density
> function of these variables is given by
>
> $$
> \begin{align*}
> f(x, y) = \begin{cases}
>             2, &0 < x < y, 0 < y < 1, \\
>             0, &\text{elsewhere}.
>           \end{cases} \tag{11.1}
> \end{align*}
> $$
>
> Find the probability density function for the amount of kerosene left
> in the tank at the end of the day.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}

Z \coloneqq Y - X, \quad W &\coloneqq Y &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{leftover kerosene $Z$, auxiliary $W$}} \\
\\
x = w - z, \quad y &= w &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{\{Walpole \S7.2, theorem 7.4\} inverse transform}} \\
J = \begin{vmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} &= -1, \quad |J| = 1 \\
\\
0 < x < y < 1 &\implies 0 < w - z, \ w - z < w, \ w < 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{transform the support}} \\
              &\implies 0 < z < w < 1 \\
\\
g(z, w) &= f(w - z, w)|J| = 2(1) = 2, \quad 0 < z < w < 1 &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{joint density of $Z, W$}} \\
\\
g(z) &= \int_z^1 2 \, dw &&
  \htmlClass{hljs-comment}{\textit{marginal density of $Z$}} \\
     &= 2(1 - z) \\
g(z) &= \begin{cases}
          2(1-z), &0 < z < 1, \\
          0,      &\text{elsewhere}.
        \end{cases} \space_\blacksquare

\end{align*}
$$

</section>
